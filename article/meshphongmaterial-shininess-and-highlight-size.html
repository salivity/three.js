MeshPhongMaterial Shininess and Highlight Size

In Three.js, the shininess property of MeshPhongMaterial controls the size and sharpness of specular reflections by acting as the power exponent in the Blinn-Phong reflection model. Increasing shininess does not simply scale down the highlight linearly; rather, it steepens the cosine curve of the specular equation, creating an inverse relationship where higher values contract the angular diameter and surface area of the bright spot.

The Blinn-Phong Formulation in Three.js

Three.js implements specular highlights using the Blinn-Phong shading model within its shader chunks. The specular intensity \(I_s\) at a given surface point is calculated using the dot product of the surface normal vector \(\mathbf{N}\) and the half-way vector \(\mathbf{H}\) (the normalized sum of the light direction vector \(\mathbf{L}\) and the view direction vector \(\mathbf{V}\)):

\[\mathbf{H} = \frac{\mathbf{L} + \mathbf{V}}{\|\mathbf{L} + \mathbf{V}\|}\]

\[I_s = (\mathbf{N} \cdot \mathbf{H})^{s}\]

In this formula, \(s\) represents the shininess property. Both \(\mathbf{N}\) and \(\mathbf{H}\) are unit vectors, meaning their dot product equals the cosine of the angle \(\theta\) between them:

\[I_s = (\cos\theta)^{s}\]

Because \(\theta\) ranges from \(0\) to \(\frac{\pi}{2}\) radians across the visible hemisphere, \(\cos\theta\) produces a value between \(1\) (direct reflection toward the viewer) and \(0\) (perpendicular or facing away).

Mathematical Effect on Highlight Radius

To quantify the visible size of the specular highlight, consider the angular radius \(\theta_{1/2}\) at which the intensity drops to half of its peak value (\(I_s = 0.5\)):

\[(\cos\theta_{1/2})^s = 0.5\]

Solving for \(\theta_{1/2}\):

\[\cos\theta_{1/2} = 0.5^{\frac{1}{s}}\]

\[\theta_{1/2} = \arccos\left(0.5^{\frac{1}{s}}\right)\]

Applying a Taylor series expansion for the cosine term (\(\cos\theta \approx 1 - \frac{\theta^2}{2}\)) and for the exponential term (\(0.5^{\frac{1}{s}} = e^{-\frac{\ln 2}{s}} \approx 1 - \frac{\ln 2}{s}\)) demonstrates the asymptotic behavior:

\[1 - \frac{\theta_{1/2}^2}{2} \approx 1 - \frac{\ln 2}{s}\]

\[\theta_{1/2} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2 \ln 2}{s}}\]

This approximation indicates that the angular radius \(\theta_{1/2}\) is proportional to the inverse square root of shininess:

\[\theta_{1/2} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\]

Effect on Highlight Area

Because the visible highlight forms a roughly circular region on a flat surface, its projected area \(A\) is proportional to the square of its angular radius:

\[A \propto \theta_{1/2}^2 \propto \frac{1}{s}\]

This establishes a directly inverse relationship between the shininess value and the surface area of the specular spot:

  • Doubling shininess cuts the approximate surface area of the highlight in half.
  • Quadrupling shininess halves the apparent angular width (diameter) of the highlight.
  • Extremely low values (e.g., \(s = 1\) to \(5\)) produce a very wide, diffuse lobe across the surface.
  • Higher values (e.g., \(s = 100\) or more) compress the highlight into a tight, pinpoint reflection characteristic of polished, glossy surfaces.
