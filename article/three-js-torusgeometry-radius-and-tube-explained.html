Three.js TorusGeometry Radius and Tube Explained

In Three.js, TorusGeometry generates a three-dimensional ring or doughnut shape governed primarily by its radius and tube parameters. This article breaks down the mathematical relationship between these two properties, detailing how they correspond to classic geometric torus definitions, dictate the dimensions of the final mesh, and determine the structural topology of the resulting shape.

Major vs. Minor Radii

In classical geometry, a torus is formed by revolving a circle around an axis coplanar with the circle. It is defined by two fundamental values:

  • Major Radius (\(R\)): The distance from the center of the torus to the center of the cross-sectional tube. In Three.js, this is the radius argument.
  • Minor Radius (\(r\)): The radius of the cross-sectional tube itself. In Three.js, this is the tube argument.

The constructor signature reflects this hierarchy:

THREE.TorusGeometry(radius, tube, radialSegments, tubularSegments, arc);

Boundary Dimensions

The overall physical boundaries of the generated geometry depend entirely on the sum and difference of the radius (\(R\)) and the tube (\(r\)):

  • Outer Radius (\(R_{outer}\)): The distance from the central axis to the outermost edge of the geometry. \[\text{Outer Radius} = \text{radius} + \text{tube}\]
  • Inner Radius (\(R_{inner}\)): The distance from the central axis to the innermost edge (the hole's boundary). \[\text{Inner Radius} = \text{radius} - \text{tube}\]
  • Total Width / Outer Diameter: The full width of the mesh along the X or Y plane. \[\text{Outer Diameter} = 2 \times (\text{radius} + \text{tube})\]
  • Hole Diameter: The diameter of the central open space. \[\text{Hole Diameter} = 2 \times (\text{radius} - \text{tube})\]
  • Total Height / Thickness: The thickness along the Z axis (the axis perpendicular to the revolution plane). \[\text{Thickness} = 2 \times \text{tube}\]

Topological States Based on the Ratio

The relationship between radius and tube determines the topological classification of the resulting 3D model:

  1. Standard Ring Torus (radius > tube): When the major radius is strictly greater than the minor radius, the inner radius is positive (\(R - r > 0\)). The torus features an open central hole. This is the standard configuration for doughnut or ring shapes.
  2. Horn Torus (radius === tube): When the major radius equals the minor radius, the inner radius becomes zero (\(R - r = 0\)). The central hole closes completely, meeting at a single tangent point along the revolution axis.
  3. Spindle Torus (radius < tube): When the tube radius exceeds the primary radius, the inner radius becomes negative (\(R - r < 0\)). The geometry self-intersects at the center, creating an overlapping core with outer cusps instead of a central hole.

Surface Area and Volume Calculations

Because Three.js constructs the vertices using standard parametric torus equations, the mathematical surface area (\(A\)) and enclosed volume (\(V\)) of the ideal smooth torus can be derived directly using radius (\(R\)) and tube (\(r\)):

  • Surface Area: \[A = 4\pi^2 \times \text{radius} \times \text{tube}\]
  • Enclosed Volume: \[V = 2\pi^2 \times \text{radius} \times \text{tube}^2\]

Maintaining an intended design scale requires modifying both parameters simultaneously. Increasing tube while holding radius constant increases both the thickness of the body and the outer diameter while shrinking the central void.
