Three.js TetrahedronGeometry Explained

In Three.js, TetrahedronGeometry serves as the most fundamental volumetric primitive, providing a lightweight foundation for 3D meshes by enclosing space using the absolute minimum number of vertices and faces. This article explains how the class functions, breaks down its constructor parameters, details its underlying vertex structure, and explores its practical applications in WebGL development.

The Simplest 3D Volumetric Primitive

In Euclidean geometry, just as a triangle is the minimal 2D shape required to enclose an area, a tetrahedron is the simplest polyhedron capable of enclosing a three-dimensional volume. It is one of the five classical convex regular polyhedra, known as Platonic solids, consisting of four triangular faces, six straight edges, and four vertex corners. Because it is mathematically impossible to enclose a 3D volume with fewer than four planar surfaces, TetrahedronGeometry represents the lowest computational footprint for any true 3D solid in the Three.js library.

Constructor Syntax and Parameters

Instantiating a tetrahedron in Three.js requires calling the TetrahedronGeometry constructor, which accepts two optional arguments:

const geometry = new THREE.TetrahedronGeometry(radius, detail);
  • radius (Float): Determines the overall scale by measuring the distance from the local origin to each of the four outer vertices. The default value is 1.
  • detail (Integer): Controls the level of subdivision applied to the geometry. The default value is 0, which generates the baseline four-faced Platonic solid. Increasing this value recursively divides each triangular face into smaller triangles and projects the resulting vertices outward to the specified radius. As detail increases, the shape progressively transforms into a geodesic sphere.

Internal Buffer and Vertex Construction

Under the hood, TetrahedronGeometry extends PolyhedronGeometry, which generates a standard Three.js BufferGeometry.

When created with a detail setting of 0, the geometry defines four equilateral triangular faces. To support correct surface shading, Three.js generates non-indexed geometry where each of the four faces contains three distinct vertices, resulting in a buffer of 12 vertices rather than 4 shared ones. This unshared layout ensures that each face has its own unique surface normal vector, producing the sharp, crisp edges characteristic of flat-shaded polyhedra when paired with lighting-dependent materials like MeshStandardMaterial or MeshPhongMaterial. UV texture coordinates are also automatically generated, mapping the 2D texture plane across the four triangular facets.

Performance Benefits and Common Use Cases

Because of its minimal complexity, TetrahedronGeometry is one of the most efficient primitives available for rendering solid forms:

  • Massive Particle Systems: When instanced using InstancedMesh, tetrahedra provide a clear sense of volume and rotation in 3D space with significantly fewer draw calls and vertex shader calculations than cubes or spheres.
  • Low-Poly Design: It serves as an ideal base geometry for stylized, angular art styles, representing crystals, rocks, space debris, or abstract geometric patterns.
  • Geodesic Generation: By scaling the detail parameter, developers can generate lightweight, uniform spherical structures without the polar distortion typical of standard latitude-longitude sphere meshes (SphereGeometry).
  • Physics Collisions: It functions as a lightweight convex hull representation for simplified physical interactions, enabling fast collision detection in interactive scenes.
