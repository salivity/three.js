Three.js generateUUID Collision Probability

Three.js relies on MathUtils.generateUUID to assign unique identifiers to meshes, materials, textures, and other core scene objects. Because this function produces standard RFC4122 Version 4 UUIDs containing 122 bits of entropy, the statistical probability of generating duplicate IDs across millions of objects is virtually zero—measuring around \(10^{-25}\) for one million objects and remaining negligible even at tens of millions.

The Mathematics of UUID v4 Entropy

A standard Version 4 UUID consists of 128 bits. Six of these bits are reserved for structural metadata: four bits signify version 4 (0100), and two bits designate the variant (10).

This leaves exactly 122 bits of unpredictable pseudo-random data:

\[\text{Total Possible Unique UUIDs} = 2^{122} \approx 5.316 \times 10^{36}\]

Because unique values are sampled with replacement, estimating collision probability requires applying the Birthday Problem approximation:

\[P(n) \approx 1 - e^{-\frac{n^2}{2N}}\]

Where:

  • \(n\) is the number of generated UUIDs.
  • \(N\) is the total number of possible combinations (\(2^{122}\)).
  • For very small values of \(\frac{n^2}{2N}\), the formula simplifies via Taylor expansion to:

\[P(n) \approx \frac{n^2}{2 \times 2^{122}} = \frac{n^2}{2^{123}}\]

Probability Across Common Thresholds

Evaluating the formula for varying object scale reveals how minuscule the risk is:

  • 1 Million Objects (\(10^6\)): \[P(10^6) \approx \frac{(10^6)^2}{2 \times 5.316 \times 10^{36}} \approx 9.4 \times 10^{-26}\] This is approximately a 1 in \(10^{25}\) chance of a single collision occurring.

  • 10 Million Objects (\(10^7\)): \[P(10^7) \approx \frac{(10^7)^2}{1.063 \times 10^{37}} \approx 9.4 \times 10^{-24}\]

  • 100 Million Objects (\(10^8\)): \[P(10^8) \approx \frac{(10^8)^2}{1.063 \times 10^{37}} \approx 9.4 \times 10^{-22}\]

To achieve even a 50% probability of a single collision (\(P = 0.5\)), an application would need to generate approximately \(2.71 \times 10^{18}\) UUIDs—equivalent to generating 1 billion UUIDs per second continuously for roughly 86 years.

PRNG Quality and Implementation Factors

The theoretical probability assumes an ideal, uniform random number distribution. In Three.js, modern versions of MathUtils.generateUUID prioritize the Web Cryptography API (crypto.randomUUID() or crypto.getRandomValues()) when available. Cryptographically secure pseudorandom number generators (CSPRNG) satisfy strict statistical randomness tests, ensuring the true collision rate matches the mathematical expectation.

In legacy or restricted JavaScript runtimes falling back to Math.random(), the entropy depends on the browser engine's internal pseudorandom implementation (typically xorshift128+ in modern V8). While non-cryptographic PRNGs can theoretically experience cycle loops or imperfect distribution over billions of cycles, they still provide sufficient spread to prevent collisions when managing millions of active scene entities in WebGL applications.
