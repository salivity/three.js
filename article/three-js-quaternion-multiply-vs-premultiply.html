Three.js Quaternion multiply vs premultiply

In Three.js, combining rotations with quaternions depends on the non-commutative nature of quaternion algebra, where the order of multiplication alters the result. The core mathematical difference between Quaternion.multiply and Quaternion.premultiply lies in which side the incoming quaternion is multiplied onto the existing one. This mathematical distinction directly dictates whether a rotation is applied relative to an object's local axes (intrinsic rotation) or the static world axes (extrinsic rotation).

Mathematical Definition

Given an initial quaternion \(A\) (this) and a second quaternion \(B\):

  • A.multiply(B) computes the post-multiplication: \[A_{\text{new}} = A \times B\]

  • A.premultiply(B) computes the pre-multiplication: \[A_{\text{new}} = B \times A\]

Because quaternion multiplication (the Hamilton product) is non-commutative, \(A \times B \neq B \times A\) in almost all practical scenarios.

Sequential Rotations and Vector Transformation

When a quaternion rotates a vector \(v\), the operation is expressed as:

\[v' = q \cdot v \cdot q^{-1}\]

If you apply rotation \(A\) first, followed by rotation \(B\), the combined transformation on vector \(v\) expands to:

\[v' = B \cdot (A \cdot v \cdot A^{-1}) \cdot B^{-1} = (B \cdot A) \cdot v \cdot (B \cdot A)^{-1}\]

Mathematically, the composite rotation quaternion representing rotation \(A\) followed by rotation \(B\) is \(B \times A\).

Local vs. World Space Interpretation

The mathematical placement of the factor determines the frame of reference for the transformation:

  1. A.premultiply(B) (\(B \times A\)): World / Extrinsic Rotation Because \(B\) is applied outside \(A\), the transformation \(B\) occurs after \(A\) with respect to the fixed global coordinate system. Use premultiply when you want to rotate an object around the scene's global axes regardless of the object's current orientation.

  2. A.multiply(B) (\(A \times B\)): Local / Intrinsic Rotation Because \(B\) is applied inside, the transformation \(B\) is evaluated in the local coordinate frame already transformed by \(A\). Use multiply when you want to rotate an object relative to its own internal axes (for example, pitching an aircraft up relative to its own nose and wings).

Summary

  • A.multiply(B) executes \(A \times B\), applying rotation \(B\) in local space.
  • A.premultiply(B) executes \(B \times A\), applying rotation \(B\) in world space.
