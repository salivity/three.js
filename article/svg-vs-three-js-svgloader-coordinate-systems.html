SVG vs Three.js SVGLoader Coordinate Systems

When importing vector graphics into a 3D scene using the Three.js SVGLoader, paths often appear inverted or displaced. This article provides a clear overview of the differences between the 2D SVG coordinate space and the 3D Three.js coordinate system, explaining why imported paths render upside down and how to correctly reorient, scale, and center the resulting geometries in 3D space.

The SVG Coordinate System

Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG) use a 2D screen-based coordinate system:

  • Origin (0, 0): Located at the top-left corner of the viewport or bounding box.
  • X-axis: Extends horizontally to the right (positive values).
  • Y-axis: Extends vertically downward (positive values).
  • Z-axis: None; it is strictly a two-dimensional format.

The Three.js Coordinate System

Three.js uses a standard right-handed 3D Cartesian coordinate system:

  • Origin (0, 0, 0): Located at the center of the local coordinate space or world space.
  • X-axis: Extends horizontally to the right (positive values).
  • Y-axis: Extends vertically upward (positive values).
  • Z-axis: Extends outward from the screen toward the viewer (positive values).

Key Differences When Using SVGLoader

When SVGLoader parses SVG path data, it reads coordinates directly from the vector definitions and maps them onto 2D Three.js shapes (ShapeGeometry or ExtrudeGeometry):

  1. Y-Axis Inversion: Because SVGLoader directly maps the SVG's downward Y coordinates to Three.js Y coordinates without altering their sign, shapes appear upside down. A point at (0, 100) in an SVG is located downward, but in Three.js, it is placed 100 units upward.
  2. Anchor Point Offset: In an SVG, elements are drawn relative to the top-left corner of the canvas. In Three.js, objects typically rotate and transform around their center. Without adjustment, the anchor point of an SVG-generated geometry remains at its top-left rather than its visual center.
  3. Scale Discrepancy: SVG coordinates are measured in pixels or arbitrary units defined by the viewBox. In Three.js, units are arbitrary world units (often treated as meters). An SVG that is 800 pixels wide will generate a geometry 800 units wide, which is often significantly larger than the camera's default view frustum.

Correcting the Discrepancies

To align SVG geometries with the standard Three.js space, apply the following adjustments after parsing:

1. Invert the Y-Axis

Rotate the parent mesh or group 180 degrees around the X-axis, or invert the Y-scale:

// Method A: Rotate around the X-axis (recommended to preserve front-facing normals)
mesh.rotation.x = Math.PI;

// Method B: Negative scale
mesh.scale.set(1, -1, 1);

2. Center the Geometry

To move the local origin from the top-left corner to the center of the shape:

geometry.computeBoundingBox();
geometry.center();

3. Normalize Scale

Scale the mesh down to fit your scene's unit scale:

const scaleFactor = 0.01;
mesh.scale.multiplyScalar(scaleFactor);
