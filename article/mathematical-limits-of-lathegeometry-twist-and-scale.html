Mathematical Limits of LatheGeometry Twist and Scale

Revolving a spline to create a 3D mesh in Three.js is fundamentally handled by sweeping a profile curve around an axis of revolution. While the standard LatheGeometry constructor natively accepts radial segments, starting angles, and arc lengths, custom extensions and parametric shaders frequently introduce scale and twist parameters along the revolution path. This article examines the theoretical, mathematical, and computational boundaries governing scale and twist, focusing on self-intersection, degenerate topology, floating-point precision, and tessellation limits.

Mathematical Definition of the Revolution

A standard lathe operation rotates an array of 2D points \((x_i, y_i)\) around the Y-axis by an angle \(\phi \in [0, \phi_{length}]\). When introducing twist (\(\tau\)) and scale (\(s\)), the transformation for a point at height \(y\) and angle \(\phi\) is defined parametrically as:

\[x' = s(y, \phi) \cdot x \cdot \cos(\phi + \tau(y, \phi))\] \[z' = s(y, \phi) \cdot x \cdot \sin(\phi + \tau(y, \phi))\] \[y' = y\]

Where:

  • \(s(y, \phi)\) is a scaling factor modifying the profile's distance from the axis.
  • \(\tau(y, \phi)\) is an angular offset (twist) added as a function of height or revolution progress.

Limits of the Scale Parameter (\(s\))

The scale parameter modifies the radial distance of vertices from the central axis. Mathematically, \(s \in (-\infty, \infty)\), but geometric validity imposes strict constraints:

  • Lower Non-Degenerate Bound (\(s > 0\)): For the generated surface to remain a manifold, \(s\) must be strictly greater than zero.
  • The Zero Singularity (\(s = 0\)): When \(s = 0\), all points collapse onto the rotation axis \((0, y, 0)\). This reduces surface area to zero, creates duplicate vertices, produces degenerate triangles with zero area, and results in undefined surface normal vectors (\(\vec{n} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{p}}{\partial \phi} \times \frac{\partial \mathbf{p}}{\partial y} = \mathbf{0}\)).
  • Negative Values (\(s < 0\)): A negative scale inverts the spline across the axis of revolution. This flips the face winding order (counter-clockwise to clockwise), causing surface normals to point inward. Without reversing index buffers, backface culling in Three.js will render the mesh invisible or visually inverted.
  • IEEE 754 Floating-Point Limits: Practically, scale is bounded below by Number.EPSILON (\(\approx 2.22 \times 10^{-16}\)). Any scale below this value causes catastrophic cancellation in vertex position shaders and normal calculations. The upper bound is constrained by numerical precision limits before vertex coordinates overflow to Infinity.

Limits of the Twist Parameter (\(\tau\))

Twist applies a rotation offset along the axis of revolution. Purely algebraically, the twist angle \(\tau\) has no upper or lower boundary and belongs to \((-\infty, \infty)\) radians. However, discrete mesh generation introduces topological boundaries:

  • The Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Limit: A mesh is discretized into a finite number of segments (\(N\)). For the geometry to retain its intended topology without self-intersecting, the relative twist between two adjacent sample points must not exceed \(\pi\) radians (\(180^\circ\)): \[\Delta \tau = |\tau_{k+1} - \tau_k| < \pi\] Exceeding this rate causes triangles to invert through themselves, creating non-manifold self-intersections and corrupted face normal interpolation.
  • Maximum Twist Relative to Segment Count: If a total twist of \(T\) radians is applied uniformly across \(N\) segments along the axis or curve length, the absolute limit before mesh inversion occurs is: \[|T| < N \cdot \pi\] To maintain smooth lighting and avoid severe shear distortion, practical visual limits require \(\Delta \tau \le \frac{\pi}{4}\) (\(45^\circ\)) per segment.
  • Aspect Ratio and Shear Degradation: As twist increases, triangles are stretched diagonally relative to the axis. In the limit as \(\tau \to \infty\), the aspect ratio of the generated faces approaches infinity, leading to floating-point rounding errors in depth-testing (Z-fighting) and failure of derivative-based normal computations in WebGL.

Parameter Interactions and Singularity Conditions

The combined limits of scale and twist depend on the Jacobian matrix of the surface mapping. A singular point occurs when the determinant of the metric tensor equals zero:

\[g = \det \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{p}_\phi \cdot \mathbf{p}_\phi & \mathbf{p}_\phi \cdot \mathbf{p}_y \\ \mathbf{p}_y \cdot \mathbf{p}_\phi & \mathbf{p}_y \cdot \mathbf{p}_y \end{bmatrix} = 0\]

If \(s \to 0\) simultaneously as \(\tau \to \infty\), the spatial derivative \(\frac{\partial \tau}{\partial y}\) shears the mesh infinitely faster than the radius shrinks. This creates a non-isolated pinch singularity that renders the geometry computationally non-manifold, preventing reliable boolean operations, physics mesh generation, and raycasting within Three.js.
