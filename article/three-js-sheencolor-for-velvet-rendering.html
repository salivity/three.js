Three.js sheenColor for Velvet Rendering

This article explains the function of the sheenColor property in Three.js's MeshPhysicalMaterial, focusing on how it enables realistic rendering of microfiber fabrics such as velvet. You will learn how sheenColor controls grazing-angle light scattering, how it interacts with the diffuse base color, and how to combine it with other sheen properties to produce accurate textile appearances in WebGL.

The Physics of Microfiber and Velvet

Woven materials like velvet, satin, and microfiber cloth do not reflect light like standard hard surfaces. Instead of producing sharp specular highlights, they feature millions of tiny, perpendicular fabric fibers that catch light primarily at grazing angles—the outer edges of the surface relative to the camera. This creates a soft, luminous rim highlight known as the sheen effect.

In Three.js, standard materials like MeshStandardMaterial lack the specialized shading model required to simulate this phenomenon. To address this, MeshPhysicalMaterial incorporates an analytical cloth shading model based on the Charlie sheen distribution.

The Specific Function of sheenColor

The sheenColor property defines the exact hue and brightness of the specular reflection observed at grazing angles. While standard specular reflections typically inherit the light source's color on non-metallic surfaces, cloth fibers disperse and tint reflected light through subsurface scattering and fiber composition.

Key functions of sheenColor include:

  • Defining Rim Light Chrominance: It dictates whether the edge glow matches the fabric's base color or introduces a complementary shift. Setting sheenColor to a slightly lighter or shifted tint compared to the base color creates the rich, deep texture characteristic of luxury velvet.
  • Simulating Two-Tone and Iridescent Fabrics: Many velvet fabrics appear darker when viewed directly and shift to a brighter, lighter shade along the silhouettes. By decoupling sheenColor from the material’s base color, developers can simulate synthetic or dual-tone weaves (such as shot silk or crushed velvet) where the fibers reflect a completely different hue than the backing weave.
  • Controlling Specular Energy per Color Channel: By modifying the RGB components of sheenColor, you control how strongly individual color wavelengths reflect off the microfibers.

Interaction with Related Properties

sheenColor does not function in isolation; it works in tandem with two other properties on MeshPhysicalMaterial:

  1. sheen (or sheenIntensity): A scalar value (typically 0.0 to 1.0) that acts as a master multiplier for the sheen effect. If sheen is set to 0, the sheenColor will have no visible effect on the render.
  2. sheenRoughness: Determines how broadly the sheen highlight spreads from the grazing edges inward across the geometry. A low roughness produces a tight, narrow rim along the extreme silhouettes, whereas a high roughness spreads the soft microfiber glow deeper into the face of the geometry, mimicking thick pile or plush velvet.

Implementation Example

To configure a realistic red velvet material using sheenColor:

const velvetMaterial = new THREE.MeshPhysicalMaterial({
    color: 0x3b0008,          // Deep, dark crimson base
    roughness: 0.8,           // High diffuse roughness
    metalness: 0.0,           // Fabrics are non-metallic
    sheen: 1.0,               // Enable full sheen strength
    sheenColor: 0xff4d6d,     // Bright, saturated pink/red rim highlight
    sheenRoughness: 0.5       // Soft spread across fiber tips
});

By explicitly setting sheenColor, you provide the physical rendering engine with the optical data needed to simulate light catching the tips of microscopic fibers, elevating flat meshes into tactile, visually convincing textiles.
