Icosahedron vs Octahedron Geometry in Three.js

In Three.js, IcosahedronGeometry and OctahedronGeometry both generate 3D meshes based on Platonic solids composed of equilateral triangular faces, but they differ fundamentally in their base mathematical topology, coordinate definitions, and subdivision densities. While an octahedron is defined by 8 faces and 6 vertices derived from orthogonal Cartesian axes, an icosahedron consists of 20 faces and 12 vertices defined using the golden ratio. Understanding these mathematical distinctions is critical when choosing a base geometry for polyhedral models or geodesic sphere approximations via the detail parameter.

Base Polyhedral Topology

Both geometries represent Platonic solids, meaning every face is an identical regular polygon, and every vertex has the same degree. However, their combinatorial properties differ:

  • OctahedronGeometry:

    • Faces: 8
    • Vertices: 6
    • Edges: 12
    • Schläfli Symbol: {3, 4} (three-sided faces, 4 meeting at each vertex)
    • Dihedral Angle: \(\approx 109.47^\circ\)

  • IcosahedronGeometry:

    • Faces: 20
    • Vertices: 12
    • Edges: 30
    • Schläfli Symbol: {3, 5} (three-sided faces, 5 meeting at each vertex)
    • Dihedral Angle: \(\approx 138.19^\circ\)

Coordinate Generation Mathematics

The mathematical formulas used by Three.js to construct the base vertices of each shape rely on different geometric symmetries.

Octahedron Coordinates

The vertices of a regular octahedron centered at the origin with a radius of \(r\) lie directly on the Cartesian coordinate axes. The 6 vertices are generated by all permutations of:

\[(\pm r, 0, 0), \quad (0, \pm r, 0), \quad (0, 0, \pm r)\]

Because the vertices align directly with orthogonal axes, the base octahedron is the dual polyhedron of the cube.

Icosahedron Coordinates

An icosahedron cannot be defined solely using standard orthogonal permutations; it requires the golden ratio, \(\phi\):

\[\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.6180339887\]

The 12 vertices of an icosahedron are constructed from three orthogonal, mutually perpendicular golden rectangles. Centered at the origin, the unscaled coordinates are cyclic permutations of:

\[(0, \pm 1, \pm \phi), \quad (\pm 1, \pm \phi, 0), \quad (\pm \phi, 0, \pm 1)\]

Three.js then normalizes these vectors and scales them by the requested radius parameter to ensure all vertices lie exactly on a sphere of radius \(r\).

Subdivision Behavior and the detail Parameter

Both constructors accept a detail parameter: OctahedronGeometry(radius, detail) and IcosahedronGeometry(radius, detail).

When detail is greater than 0, Three.js recursively splits each original triangular face into four smaller triangles and projects the new vertices onto the bounding sphere of the specified radius.

The formula for the total number of faces at subdivision level \(n\) (where \(n = \text{detail}\)) is:

\[\text{Faces} = F_{\text{base}} \times 4^n\]

  • Octahedron: \(\text{Faces} = 8 \times 4^n\)
    • Detail 0: 8 faces
    • Detail 1: 32 faces
    • Detail 2: 128 faces
  • Icosahedron: \(\text{Faces} = 20 \times 4^n\)
    • Detail 0: 20 faces
    • Detail 1: 80 faces
    • Detail 2: 320 faces

Practical Geometric Implications

Because the base icosahedron has more vertices and a larger dihedral angle (\(138.19^\circ\) versus \(109.47^\circ\)), its initial shape is closer to a sphere than an octahedron. Subdividing an icosahedron results in a more uniform distribution of vertices across the spherical surface, avoiding the structural clustering and distortion that occurs along the octahedral axes. Consequently, IcosahedronGeometry is mathematically superior for generating smooth geodesic spheres, while OctahedronGeometry is optimal when lower polygon counts or octahedral symmetry alignments are required.
