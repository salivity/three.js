Transient Input vs Tracked Controller in WebXR

In Three.js WebXR, the primary difference between a transient input and a standard tracked controller lies in their lifecycle and how their spatial poses are tracked. While standard tracked controllers are persistent physical devices that continuously report position and orientation, transient inputs are temporary, event-driven interactions—such as screen taps in mobile augmented reality—that exist only for the duration of an action.

Standard Tracked Controllers

Standard tracked controllers represent physical hardware such as the Meta Quest Touch controllers, HTC Vive wands, or Valve Index controllers.

  • Persistence: These devices maintain an active connection and continuous presence in the WebXR session. They exist in the scene even when the user is not actively pressing buttons or interacting with virtual objects.
  • Tracking Modes: They typically provide 6DoF (Degrees of Freedom) tracking, continuously updating both their target ray space (for pointing) and grip space (for rendering 3D controller models or virtual hands).
  • Three.js Handling: Developers access these controllers using renderer.xr.getController(index) and renderer.xr.getControllerGrip(index). Because their poses update every frame, you can reliably attach persistent visual models, laser pointers, or physics colliders to them throughout the session.

Transient Inputs

Transient inputs correspond to WebXR input sources classified under the transient-pointer target ray mode. Common examples include a user tapping on the screen of an AR-enabled smartphone, gaze-and-tap mechanisms, or temporary pinch gestures.

  • Lifecycle: Unlike standard controllers, transient inputs do not continuously track a physical object in space. The input source is established dynamically when an action begins (such as a finger touching the screen) and terminates as soon as the action ends (lifting the finger).
  • Tracking Constraints: A transient input provides a ray pose only while the interaction is active. Because there is no persistent physical controller in the user's hand, these inputs do not have an associated grip space, making it impossible to render a permanent controller model in the virtual world.
  • Three.js Handling: While Three.js maps these inputs through the same renderer.xr.getController() index system, their tracking data is transient. Interaction logic relies heavily on listening for selectstart, select, and selectend events. When an event fires, Three.js updates the controller’s matrix to the location where the tap or interaction occurred, typically using it to cast a ray into the scene at that specific moment.

Key Architectural Differences

  1. Target Ray Mode: Standard controllers use the tracked-pointer mode, indicating continuous spatial tracking independent of user actions. Transient inputs use the transient-pointer mode, indicating that the pointing ray is short-lived and valid primarily during active input events.
  2. Visual Representation: Standard controllers warrant persistent 3D models and continuous reticles. Transient inputs require reactive visuals, such as an indicator that appears at the point of contact only when a tap or gesture occurs.
  3. Use Case Alignment: Standard controllers are designed for immersive VR and MR experiences requiring continuous manipulation, tool holding, and spatial navigation. Transient inputs are optimized for mobile AR and basic viewer devices where interaction is sporadic and screen- or gaze-dependent.
