Three.js Matrix3 getNormalMatrix Explained

This article provides a concise overview of the specific mathematical operation performed by the Matrix3.getNormalMatrix method in Three.js. It covers why surface normals require a specialized transformation matrix rather than the standard model-view matrix, details the mathematical derivation involving the inverse transpose, and outlines the step-by-step calculation executed internally by the Three.js library.

The Mathematical Function

The specific mathematical function of Matrix3.getNormalMatrix is to calculate the inverse transpose of the upper-left 3×3 submatrix of a given 4×4 affine transformation matrix (typically the modelViewMatrix or modelMatrix).

Mathematically, if \(M\) represents the 4×4 transformation matrix, and \(A\) represents the upper-left 3×3 submatrix of \(M\) (containing the rotation and scaling components):

\[N = (A^{-1})^T\]

Where:

  • \(A\) is the 3×3 rotation-scale matrix extracted from the input Matrix4.
  • \(A^{-1}\) is the matrix inverse of \(A\).
  • \(T\) denotes the transpose operation.
  • \(N\) is the resulting 3×3 normal matrix.

In Three.js source code, this is implemented directly as:

this.setFromMatrix4( matrix4 ).invert().transpose();

Why the Inverse Transpose is Required

Surface normals are directional vectors that must remain perpendicular to the surface of a polygon. While translations, rotations, and uniform scaling preserve the perpendicularity of normals when using standard matrix multiplication, non-uniform scaling and shearing distort angles.

If a mesh is scaled along only one axis (for example, stretched along the X-axis), applying that same scale matrix directly to the surface normals tilts them incorrectly, causing them to lose their 90-degree angle relative to the surface tangent vectors.

The Mathematical Proof

To understand why the inverse transpose is necessary, consider a tangent vector \(\mathbf{t}\) on a surface and the corresponding surface normal \(\mathbf{n}\). Because they are perpendicular, their dot product equals zero:

\[\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{t} = \mathbf{n}^T \mathbf{t} = 0\]

When an object is transformed by matrix \(A\), the tangent vector transforms directly:

\[\mathbf{t}' = A \mathbf{t}\]

The transformed normal \(\mathbf{n}'\) must be computed using an unknown matrix \(N\) such that \(\mathbf{n}' = N \mathbf{n}\), while still remaining orthogonal to \(\mathbf{t}'\):

\[(\mathbf{n}')^T \mathbf{t}' = 0\]

Substitute the transformed vectors into the equation:

\[(N \mathbf{n})^T (A \mathbf{t}) = 0\]

Using the transpose property \((A B)^T = B^T A^T\):

\[\mathbf{n}^T N^T A \mathbf{t} = 0\]

For this equation to hold true for any arbitrary tangent and normal where \(\mathbf{n}^T \mathbf{t} = 0\), the product \(N^T A\) must reduce to the identity matrix \(I\):

\[N^T A = I\]

Solving for \(N\):

\[N^T = A^{-1}\]

\[N = (A^{-1})^T\]

Three.js Implementation Steps

When calling normalMatrix.getNormalMatrix( matrix4 ), Three.js executes three primary steps:

  1. Extraction (setFromMatrix4): Discards the fourth row and fourth column of the 4×4 input matrix. This removes translation components and perspective projections, leaving only rotation, scale, and shear.
  2. Inversion (invert): Inverts the 3×3 matrix. Scaling factors are inverted (e.g., a scale factor of 2 becomes 0.5), which counteracts the distortion caused by non-uniform scaling.
  3. Transposition (transpose): Flips the matrix along its main diagonal (swapping rows and columns). This reorients the directional components correctly so the resulting vector remains orthogonal to the transformed surface.
