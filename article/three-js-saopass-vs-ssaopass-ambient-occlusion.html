Three.js SAOPass vs SSAOPass Ambient Occlusion

In Three.js, ambient occlusion post-processing passes enhance scene depth by darkening creases, crevices, and intersecting surfaces. While both SSAOPass (Screen Space Ambient Occlusion) and SAOPass (Scalable Ambient Obscurance) serve this purpose, they produce distinctly different visual results: standard SSAOPass produces higher-contrast, granular contact shadows, whereas SAOPass generates smoother, broader, and more diffuse shadowing with fewer noisy artifacts.

Shadow Softness and Falloff

The most noticeable visual difference lies in how ambient shadows transition into lit surfaces.

  • SSAOPass: Employs a fixed-radius spherical or hemispherical sampling method. This yields high-contrast, tight, and harsh shadows concentrated in immediate corners and creases. The falloff can feel sudden, often giving the scene a gritty, high-contrast look.
  • SAOPass: Implements McGuire et al.’s Scalable Ambient Obscurance algorithm, which replaces binary occlusion tests with continuous obscurance falloff functions. Visually, this creates soft, gradient-rich shadows that spread across wider radii without losing geometric definition.

Noise and Grain Patterns

Because screen-space algorithms rely on randomized sample vectors to maintain performance, they inherently introduce visual noise that must be filtered out.

  • SSAOPass: Relies on a tiled noise texture paired with a post-blur pass. In complex geometry or high-frequency normal maps, this often leaves a noticeable dithered, stippled, or grainy pattern, particularly at medium distances.
  • SAOPass: Uses depth-aware filtering and spiral sampling patterns designed to eliminate high-frequency noise prior to the bilateral blur pass. The resulting occlusion appears uniformly smooth and paint-like, avoiding the static grain commonly visible when the camera moves in an SSAO-shaded scene.

Silhouette Halos and Depth Discontinuities

When an object in the foreground stands against a distant background, screen-space techniques struggle to distinguish between a valid crevice and an open spatial gap.

  • SSAOPass: Often creates dark halos or over-darkened "shadow bleed" around the silhouette edges of foreground meshes against distant geometry.
  • SAOPass: Evaluates surface normals and distance thresholds more progressively. As a result, it drastically reduces dark edge bleeding around silhouettes, leaving cleaner object borders against open space.

Visual Aesthetic Comparison

Choosing between the two passes visually depends on the desired art style:

  • Use SSAOPass when a stylized, dramatic, or high-contrast visual style is required where tight crevice accents (contact shadowing) matter more than broad, soft light transport.
  • Use SAOPass when striving for photorealism, soft indirect lighting, and clean, flicker-free rendering on smooth surfaces like architecture, product designs, or expansive terrain.
