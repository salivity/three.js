Euler Order and Gimbal Lock in Three.js

In Three.js, rotational transformations depend entirely on the sequence in which axis rotations are calculated and applied. While Three.js defaults to an 'XYZ' Euler order, explicitly defining an alternate order like 'YXZ' changes the underlying matrix multiplication sequence. This change dictates which rotational axis occupies the vulnerable "middle" position where Gimbal lock occurs, allowing developers to tailor rotation behavior to specific use cases—such as first-person cameras—and prevent axes from collapsing into one another.

How Euler Rotations Are Evaluated Mathematically

Euler rotations represent orientation through three sequential rotations around specific axes. In Three.js, rotations are intrinsic, meaning each subsequent rotation occurs around the object's newly reoriented local coordinate system.

When you define an Euler order such as 'YXZ', Three.js evaluates the transformations sequentially:

  1. Rotate around the local Y-axis (Yaw)
  2. Rotate around the modified local X-axis (Pitch)
  3. Rotate around the modified local Z-axis (Roll)

Mathematically, this translates to multiplying elemental rotation matrices. Because matrix multiplication is non-commutative (\(A \times B \neq B \times A\)), the order directly alters the resulting transformation matrix (\(R\)):

\[R = R_Y \times R_X \times R_Z\]

Altering the string value of Euler.order alters the construction sequence of this transformation matrix before it is uploaded to the GPU or combined with position and scale in the object's matrixWorld.

The Mechanism of Gimbal Lock

Gimbal lock is the mathematical loss of one degree of rotational freedom. It occurs when a rotation of \(\pm 90^\circ\) (\(\pi/2\) radians) around the middle axis in the sequence aligns the first axis with the third axis.

Under the default 'XYZ' configuration:

  • Rotation occurs around X, then Y, then Z.
  • If the middle axis (Y) rotates by \(90^\circ\), the local Z-axis rotates directly into alignment with the initial X-axis.
  • As a result, rotating around X or Z produces the exact same motion in world space. You lose the ability to rotate independently across three distinct planes.

How 'YXZ' Solves Gimbal Lock for Cameras

In applications like first-person shooters (FPS), character controllers, and architectural walkthroughs, users primarily perform horizontal panning (Yaw around Y) and vertical tilting (Pitch around X), while bank or roll (Roll around Z) is typically locked to zero.

When using the default 'XYZ' order for an FPS camera:

  • Pitching the camera up or down affects subsequent yaw calculations, often causing the camera to twist uncontrollably or freeze when looking directly upward or downward.

By explicitly setting the order to 'YXZ':

camera.rotation.order = 'YXZ';
  1. Y is applied first: The camera yaws horizontally around the global-up vector without being influenced by pitch.
  2. X is the middle axis: Vertical pitch is applied second. Even if the camera pitches \(90^\circ\) straight up or down, Gimbal lock only aligns the first axis (Y) with the third axis (Z).
  3. Z is applied last: Because standard camera controls do not apply roll (\(Z = 0\)), the alignment between the Y and Z axes does not degrade user control. The yaw movement remains fully decoupled from pitch.

Explicitly controlling the Euler order allows you to place the axis least likely to conflict—or the axis reserved for unused degrees of freedom—into the sequence positions where mathematical alignment cannot disrupt the intended user interaction.
