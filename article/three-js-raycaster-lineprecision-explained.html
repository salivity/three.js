Three.js Raycaster linePrecision Explained

In Three.js, the linePrecision property (implemented as raycaster.params.Line.threshold in modern versions) defines the maximum distance tolerance used to detect intersections between a ray and a Line or LineSegments object. Because geometric lines and mathematical rays possess zero thickness, an exact intersection is virtually impossible to achieve in a 3D environment. Setting this threshold creates a virtual cylindrical collision volume around the line, directly determining how forgiving or sensitive mouse picking and raycasting are when interacting with line elements.

The Zero-Thickness Problem

In computer graphics, a standard ray is an infinitely thin vector, and a THREE.Line consists of infinitely thin line segments connecting vertices. Mathematically, the probability of an infinitely thin ray passing precisely through an infinitely thin segment in continuous 3D space is practically zero. Without a tolerance parameter, raycasting against lines would consistently fail, making interactive features like hovering, selecting, or clicking on lines impossible.

How Intersection Detection Operates

When the raycaster checks for intersections against a line object, it calculates the shortest distance between the ray and each line segment.

  1. Distance Calculation: The raycaster computes the point of closest approach between the ray and the line segment.
  2. Threshold Comparison: If the minimum distance between the ray and the line segment is less than or equal to the precision value, an intersection is registered.
  3. Intersection Point Generation: The returned intersection point is calculated as the point on the line segment closest to the ray.

linePrecision vs. params.Line.threshold

In early versions of Three.js, this tolerance was controlled directly via raycaster.linePrecision. In current releases, Three.js organizes raycasting settings inside the params object:

// Modern Three.js syntax
raycaster.params.Line.threshold = 0.1;

// Legacy syntax (older Three.js builds)
raycaster.linePrecision = 0.1;

Both properties configure the same underlying mechanism: the collision radius around line segments measured in world units.

The Effect of Modifying the Value

Adjusting this property produces distinct behavioral changes:

  • Increasing the Value: Expanding the threshold widens the invisible hit area around the line. This makes lines significantly easier to click or hover over, especially on high-DPI screens, mobile touch interfaces, or when lines are thin. However, setting the value too high causes false positives, where intersections trigger when the cursor is noticeably far from the target line, or causes overlapping lines to conflict.
  • Decreasing the Value: Lowering the threshold tightens the hit boundary, requiring the user's cursor or ray to align closely with the rendered segment. If the value is too low, clicking lines becomes frustrating or entirely unresponsive.

Practical Considerations

The precision value is defined in world coordinate units, not screen pixels. Consequently, its effectiveness depends on camera projection and distance:

  • Perspective Views: A line positioned far from a perspective camera appears smaller on screen, meaning a fixed world-space threshold covers a larger portion of screen space. Conversely, close-up lines require more precise cursor placement.
  • Scene Scale: If your scene is modeled in millimeters, a threshold of 1 represents a single millimeter. If the scene is modeled in kilometers, that same value represents a kilometer. Always scale the threshold relative to the dimensions and camera distances used in your scene.
