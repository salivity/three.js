Three.js DecalGeometry: Projecting Decals on Meshes

The DecalGeometry class in Three.js provides a specialized solution for projecting 2D textures—such as bullet impacts, footprints, blood splatters, or decorative stickers—directly onto complex, irregular 3D surfaces. Instead of modifying the target object's original texture or relying on flat planes that clip through curved geometry, DecalGeometry computes a custom sub-mesh derived from the target mesh. This article explains the technical purpose of DecalGeometry, the rendering problems it solves, and how it functions within a Three.js scene.

The Purpose of DecalGeometry

In 3D graphics, applying localized details to a surface using simple textured quads presents significant visual artifacts. If a flat plane is placed on a curved or uneven surface, parts of the plane will hover in empty space while other parts intersect and disappear inside the mesh.

DecalGeometry solves this by functioning as an oriented bounding box projector. It captures the exact surface contours of the target mesh within a specified area, clipping the target's polygons to create a brand-new geometry that conforms perfectly to the underlying model.

Key Problems Solved by DecalGeometry

  1. Surface Conformance: Standard geometric primitives cannot adapt to arbitrary, non-flat topologies. DecalGeometry dynamically extracts the vertices and faces of the target mesh within its projection volume, wrapping seamlessly over curves, corners, and seams.
  2. Preventing Z-Fighting: When two coplanar surfaces share the exact same depth, rendering engines struggle to determine which surface is in front, causing flickering artifacts known as z-fighting. DecalGeometry, combined with material settings such as polygonOffset, slightly shifts the depth values of the decal to ensure clean rendering on top of the base mesh.
  3. Preserving Original Textures and UVs: Without decals, localized marks would require dynamically painting onto the base object's texture map (canvas baking) or editing the original UV layout. DecalGeometry generates its own local UV coordinates ranging from (0,0) to (1,1) across the projection box, leaving the underlying mesh and its materials completely untouched.

How DecalGeometry Works

Creating a decal typically involves a combination of raycasting and projection math:

  • Intersection: A raycaster identifies the point of contact on the target mesh, returning the exact hit position and face normal (surface direction).
  • Projection Box Definition: The developer defines a projection volume by specifying the target mesh, the contact position, an orientation vector derived from the normal, and dimensions representing the width, height, and depth of the decal.
  • Polygon Clipping: DecalGeometry traverses the target mesh's triangles, clips any geometry falling outside the projection volume, and outputs a new BufferGeometry.
  • Mesh Generation: The resulting geometry is paired with a material (typically using transparency and an alpha map) and added to the scene as an independent mesh.

Common Applications

  • Dynamic Combat Effects: Bullet holes, scorch marks, and slash effects placed dynamically during gameplay.
  • Environmental Details: Puddles, tire tracks, graffiti, and dirt overlays applied to static world geometry.
  • Product Customization: Adding logos, labels, or stickers to 3D product models in web-based configurators.
