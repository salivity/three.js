RectAreaLight with MeshPhongMaterial in Three.js

In Three.js, lighting components and material shaders are deeply interconnected, requiring specific combinations to function correctly. This article explores the core limitation of using a RectAreaLight with a MeshPhongMaterial, explains the technical reasons why they do not interact, and outlines how to resolve this incompatibility using appropriate physically based materials.

The Specific Limitation

The primary limitation of using a RectAreaLight with a MeshPhongMaterial is that the light will not illuminate the material at all. RectAreaLight completely ignores MeshPhongMaterial. Any mesh utilizing this material will remain unlit and appear pitch black under a RectAreaLight, unless other compatible light sources (such as a DirectionalLight, PointLight, or AmbientLight) exist within the scene.

Why This Incompatibility Exists

Three.js implements area lighting using an approximation technique known as Linearly Transformed Cosines (LTC). This algorithm allows the renderer to compute accurate diffuse and specular reflections from rectangular emitters in real time.

However, the shader routines required for LTC are only integrated into Three.js's physically based rendering (PBR) materials. MeshPhongMaterial relies on an older, non-physically based Blinn-Phong shading model. Because the Blinn-Phong shader lacks the necessary mathematical implementations and internal uniform hooks to interpret polygonal light shapes, the renderer simply skips area light calculations for this material entirely.

How to Fix the Issue

To use RectAreaLight effectively in a Three.js scene, you must adjust either the material or the light type:

  1. Switch to a Supported Material: Replace MeshPhongMaterial with MeshStandardMaterial or MeshPhysicalMaterial. These PBR materials natively support rectangular area lighting.
  2. Initialize Area Light Uniforms: When using RectAreaLight, ensure you import and call RectAreaLightUniformsLib.init() before rendering to bind the required LTC lookup tables to the PBR shaders.
  3. Use Alternative Lights for Phong Shading: If your project requires MeshPhongMaterial for performance or stylized visuals, simulate an area light by using an array of PointLight or SpotLight objects instead.
