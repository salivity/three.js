Three.js ConvexObjectBreaker for Destructible Objects

The ConvexObjectBreaker class in Three.js is a specialized utility designed to handle dynamic 3D mesh fragmentation for real-time destruction physics. By systematically slicing convex geometries along clipping planes, it splits a single object into smaller, solid fragments while preserving structural and collision integrity. When paired with a physics engine such as Ammo.js, it provides the core mathematical and geometric foundation necessary to simulate realistic shattering, breaking, and crumbling effects.

Geometric Slicing and Watertight Mesh Generation

The primary function of ConvexObjectBreaker is to divide a convex 3D mesh into two or more distinct sub-meshes using arbitrary mathematical planes (THREE.Plane). Unlike basic surface clipping, simply cutting polygons leaves an open, hollow boundary. ConvexObjectBreaker automatically seals the cross-section along the cut, creating new interior faces and UV coordinates. This ensures that every resulting fragment remains a closed, watertight (manifold) convex hull, which is visually convincing when objects shatter into pieces.

Optimization for Physics Engines

Physics engines like Ammo.js and Bullet perform collision detection exponentially faster and more reliably on convex shapes than on concave or arbitrary non-manifold meshes. By guaranteeing that each generated piece remains strictly convex, ConvexObjectBreaker allows developers to immediately assign standard convex hull collision shapes (btConvexHullShape) to the newly created fragments without requiring complex decomposition algorithms. This keeps real-time performance stable, even when dozens of debris pieces are created simultaneously.

Management of Breakable States

Beyond raw geometry manipulation, the class maintains metadata tracking the lifecycle of destructible objects. Through its internal methods, such as prepareBreakableObject(), it records properties like the object's fracture tolerance, remaining break depth, and whether a piece has become too small to be fractured further. When an impact exceeds a predetermined force threshold, the breaker evaluates these properties to determine if fragmentation should occur and prevents endless subdivision that could crash the application.

Implementation Workflow

In a typical Three.js destruction pipeline, ConvexObjectBreaker operates through the following steps:

  1. Initialization: The target mesh is marked as breakable and registered with the breaker utility.
  2. Impact Detection: The physics engine detects a collision that exceeds the object's durability limit and calculates the point and angle of impact.
  3. Plane Calculation: One or more clipping planes are generated at the point of impact, often randomized slightly to produce natural-looking debris patterns.
  4. Subdivision: The breaker's subdivideByPlanes() method splits the original geometry and outputs the new fragment geometries.
  5. Physics Instantiation: The original rigid body is removed from the physics world, and new dynamic rigid bodies are instantiated for each generated fragment, inheriting appropriate velocities and impulses from the collision.
