Fix Three.js Shadow Clipping with Camera Frustum

Shadow clipping in Three.js occurs when 3D objects fall outside the boundary of the light's shadow-casting camera. This article explains how to identify shadow clipping issues using camera helpers and adjust the shadow camera's frustum properties—specifically left, right, top, bottom, near, and far—to ensure shadows render completely and accurately.

Understanding the Cause

Three.js generates shadows by rendering the scene from the perspective of the light source using a hidden camera. For a DirectionalLight, this is an OrthographicCamera. If your scene's objects or the surfaces receiving shadows are outside the boundaries (the frustum) of this shadow camera, the shadows will look cut off, truncated, or will disappear entirely.

Step 1: Visualize the Shadow Frustum

The most efficient way to fix clipping is to see the boundaries of the shadow camera. You can do this by adding a CameraHelper to your scene.

const light = new THREE.DirectionalLight(0xffffff, 1);
light.castShadow = true;
scene.add(light);

// Create and add the helper to the scene
const shadowHelper = new THREE.CameraHelper(light.shadow.camera);
scene.add(shadowHelper);

This helper renders a wireframe box showing the exact volume where shadows are calculated. If your objects are outside this box, their shadows will clip.

Step 2: Adjust the Camera Frustum Bounds

For a DirectionalLight, you need to adjust the orthographic bounds of light.shadow.camera. Increase these values until the wireframe helper completely encloses all shadow-casting and shadow-receiving objects in your scene.

// Widen the shadow camera's reach
light.shadow.camera.left = -10;
light.shadow.camera.right = 10;
light.shadow.camera.top = 10;
light.shadow.camera.bottom = -10;

// Adjust depth boundaries
light.shadow.camera.near = 0.5; // Default is 0.5
light.shadow.camera.far = 50;   // Default is 500

Keep the bounds as tight as possible around your active scene area. Making the frustum excessively large will degrade shadow quality because the shadow map texture resolution will be stretched over a larger physical area.

Step 3: Update the Projection Matrix

Whenever you modify the frustum properties of the shadow camera dynamically in your code, you must trigger an update of its projection matrix for the changes to take effect.

light.shadow.camera.updateProjectionMatrix();
shadowHelper.update(); // Update the visual helper to match

Step 4: Fine-Tune Shadow Map Resolution

After resizing the frustum, if your shadows look pixelated or blocky, you can increase the size of the shadow map to match the new scale of your camera frustum.

light.shadow.mapSize.width = 2048;  // Default is 512
light.shadow.mapSize.height = 2048; // Default is 512

Once you have successfully adjusted the boundaries and the clipping is resolved, you can remove the CameraHelper from your production code.
