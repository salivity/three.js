Why You Should Not Scale a Three.js PerspectiveCamera

In Three.js, the PerspectiveCamera inherits a scale vector from the base Object3D class, but modifying it is a common anti-pattern that leads to unexpected rendering artifacts and broken spatial calculations. This article explains how scaling affects a camera's underlying transformation matrices, why it causes critical issues with raycasting and shaders, and how to properly alter a camera's field of view or zoom level using standard Three.js methods.

How the Scale Property Affects a Camera

Because PerspectiveCamera extends Object3D, it automatically includes properties for position, rotation, quaternion, and scale. When you modify camera.scale, Three.js applies that scaling factor to the camera's local transformation matrix (camera.matrix) and subsequent world matrix (camera.matrixWorld).

A camera in 3D graphics does not render itself; instead, it defines the transformation that maps the 3D world into the camera's local coordinate space (the view matrix). The view matrix is calculated by taking the mathematical inverse of the camera's matrixWorld:

\[\text{View Matrix} = (\text{Camera MatrixWorld})^{-1}\]

If you apply a scale of (2, 2, 2) to your camera, its matrixWorld expands, meaning the resulting view matrix scales the entire rendered scene down by half (0.5, 0.5, 0.5). Conversely, scaling the camera down causes the entire rendered world to appear larger. If non-uniform scaling is applied—such as (1, 2, 1)—the world is squashed or stretched unevenly.

Why Modifying Camera Scale Is Avoided

While scaling a camera might initially seem like a shortcut to zoom in or out, it introduces severe architectural and computational problems throughout a Three.js application.

1. It Does Not Affect the Projection Matrix

A camera's visual characteristics—such as perspective distortion, depth limits, and field of view—are governed strictly by its projectionMatrix. Modifying camera.scale completely bypasses the projection matrix and alters the view matrix instead. This creates a disconnect between the camera's optical parameters (fov, aspect, near, far) and its actual rendered output.

2. Broken Raycasting and Interaction

Three.js uses Raycaster for mouse picking, object selection, and collision detection. The raycasting algorithm relies on camera.projectionMatrix and camera.matrixWorld to unproject screen coordinates back into 3D world space.

When a camera has non-unit scale values (anything other than (1, 1, 1)), the inverted transformation matrices produce incorrect directional vectors and origin points. As a result, raycasting fails to detect objects accurately beneath the mouse cursor.

3. Shader and Lighting Inconsistencies

Many standard materials and custom shaders perform lighting and reflection calculations in view space (camera space). These calculations rely on surface normal vectors remaining unit length and orthogonal. When a camera is scaled non-uniformly, the view matrix distorts these normal vectors, causing broken specular highlights, incorrect normal mapping, and erratic shading.

4. Hierarchical Transform Pitfalls

If a camera is attached as a child to another moving or scaling object (such as a vehicle or character mesh), any scale applied to the parent object propagates down into the camera's matrixWorld. This causes unintentional scaling bugs that can be difficult to track down if developers are unaware of how scale affects the view matrix.

The Correct Ways to Zoom or Adjust Framing

Instead of altering the scale property, Three.js provides dedicated parameters designed to control camera perspective and framing without breaking the underlying transformation math.

Adjusting Field of View (FOV)

To create a natural optical zoom (similar to adjusting the focal length on a physical camera lens), change the fov property. Whenever fov is altered, you must call updateProjectionMatrix() to recompute the projection parameters:

camera.fov = 30; // Narrower field of view (zoomed in)
camera.updateProjectionMatrix();

Adjusting the Zoom Property

Three.js also provides a zoom property directly on PerspectiveCamera. This applies a uniform factor to the projection matrix without touching the view matrix:

camera.zoom = 2; // 2x magnification
camera.updateProjectionMatrix();

Translating the Camera Position

To move closer to or further away from a target without changing the optical characteristics or perspective lines of the scene, modify the camera's position or use dedicated controls like OrbitControls:

camera.position.z -= 5; // Move physically closer

Keeping the camera's scale vector strictly at (1, 1, 1) ensures that rendering pipelines, interaction tools, and mathematical calculations remain accurate and predictable.
