Fix Three.js AmbientLight Washing Out Shadows

In Three.js, AmbientLight illuminates all surfaces uniformly without direction or occlusion, which can quickly overpower the contrast created by a DirectionalLight and make shadows appear faint or washed out. To maintain crisp, realistic shadows while preserving visibility in unlit areas, you must properly balance the intensity ratio between the lights, consider color contrast, and configure the renderer's tone mapping to maintain dynamic range.

1. Maintain a Proper Intensity Ratio

The primary reason shadows wash out is an excessively high AmbientLight.intensity. Ambient light is meant only to simulate subtle, indirect bounced light, not to act as a primary light source.

  • Keep AmbientLight intensity between 0.05 and 0.25.
  • Set DirectionalLight intensity significantly higher, typically between 1.0 and 3.0 (or higher depending on your renderer's tone mapping and physical light settings).
  • A general rule of thumb is an intensity ratio of roughly 1:5 to 1:10 (Ambient to Directional).
// Weak ambient light to lift pure black values slightly
const ambientLight = new THREE.AmbientLight(0xffffff, 0.15);
scene.add(ambientLight);

// Strong primary light to produce defined shadows
const dirLight = new THREE.DirectionalLight(0xffffff, 1.5);
dirLight.position.set(5, 10, 7);
dirLight.castShadow = true;
scene.add(dirLight);

2. Use Contrasting Color Temperatures

Using pure white (0xffffff) for both lights often reduces visual depth. Shifting the ambient light toward a darker, cooler tone (such as slate gray or deep blue) simulates natural sky bounce and deepens shadows without making them completely black:

// Cool, muted ambient tone
const ambientLight = new THREE.AmbientLight(0x334455, 0.2);
scene.add(ambientLight);

// Warm directional sunlight
const dirLight = new THREE.DirectionalLight(0xfffaed, 1.8);
dirLight.position.set(10, 20, 10);
dirLight.castShadow = true;
scene.add(dirLight);

3. Consider Using HemisphereLight Instead

A flat AmbientLight illuminates the undersides of objects with the exact same strength as the sides facing the sky. A THREE.HemisphereLight provides a gradient between a sky color and a ground color, creating natural environmental occlusion that preserves shadow contrast much better than standard ambient light:

// Sky color, ground color, intensity
const hemiLight = new THREE.HemisphereLight(0xffffff, 0x222222, 0.2);
scene.add(hemiLight);

4. Enable Tone Mapping on the WebGLRenderer

Without tone mapping, high-intensity lights clip to pure white, compressing midtones and making shadows look artificially faded relative to the rest of the scene. Configuring modern tone mapping preserves dynamic range across both dark and bright areas:

renderer.toneMapping = THREE.ACESFilmicToneMapping;
renderer.toneMappingExposure = 1.0;
renderer.shadowMap.enabled = true;
renderer.shadowMap.type = THREE.PCFSoftShadowMap;

5. Tighten the Shadow Camera Bounds

If shadow borders appear faint or blurry, the directional light's shadow camera frustum might be too large, diluting shadow map resolution:

dirLight.shadow.mapSize.width = 2048;
dirLight.shadow.mapSize.height = 2048;

// Keep the shadow camera frustum as close to your scene bounds as possible
dirLight.shadow.camera.near = 0.5;
dirLight.shadow.camera.far = 50;
dirLight.shadow.camera.left = -10;
dirLight.shadow.camera.right = 10;
dirLight.shadow.camera.top = 10;
dirLight.shadow.camera.bottom = -10;
dirLight.shadow.bias = -0.0005;

By constraining the ambient light's intensity and color while letting the directional light handle the primary illumination, shadows will retain high contrast without falling into absolute black.
