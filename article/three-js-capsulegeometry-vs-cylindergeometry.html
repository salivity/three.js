Three.js CapsuleGeometry vs CylinderGeometry

This article explores the mathematical structure behind Three.js's CapsuleGeometry and compares it directly with CylinderGeometry. You will learn how the capsule is mathematically constructed as a spherocylinder, how its continuous surface normals differ from standard cylindrical meshes, and the specific application scenarios—such as physics engines, character controllers, and smooth lighting workflows—where choosing a capsule provides distinct technical advantages.

Mathematical Structure of CapsuleGeometry

A CapsuleGeometry represents a geometric shape known mathematically as a spherocylinder or a stadium of revolution.

At its core, a capsule is defined as the set of all points in three-dimensional space whose Euclidean distance to a line segment \(L\) is less than or equal to a given radius \(r\):

\[\{ \mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid \min_{\mathbf{x} \in L} \|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{x}\| \le r \}\]

In terms of mathematical morphology, this represents the Minkowski sum of a line segment of length \(h\) and a solid sphere of radius \(r\):

\[C = L \oplus B_r(0)\]

In Three.js, CapsuleGeometry(radius, length, capSegments, radialSegments) translates this continuous definition into a polygonal mesh composed of three merged regions:

  1. A central cylindrical tube: A ruled surface generated by revolving a straight line segment parallel to the central axis.
  2. Two hemispherical caps: The top and bottom closures, mathematically identical to the upper and lower halves of a standard UV sphere of radius \(r\).

The vertices are laid out along longitudinal and latitudinal steps. Unlike a cylinder, where the top and bottom caps are flat disks planar to the \(XZ\)-plane, the vertices of the capsule's caps follow trigonometric offsets based on polar angles \(\phi \in [0, \pi/2]\) and azimuthal angles \(\theta \in [0, 2\pi]\):

\[x = r \sin(\phi) \cos(\theta)\] \[z = r \sin(\phi) \sin(\theta)\] \[y = \pm \frac{h}{2} + r \cos(\phi)\]

Because the derivative of the hemispherical cap matches the derivative of the cylinder at their boundary (\(\phi = \pi/2\)), the capsule achieves \(C^1\) geometric continuity across the transition seams.

Key Differences from CylinderGeometry

While both geometries share an elongated cylindrical body, they differ significantly in their end caps and vertex normals:

  • End-Cap Topology: CylinderGeometry terminates abruptly with flat, circular disks created by triangle fans or radial rings. CapsuleGeometry terminates with smoothly curved hemispheres.
  • Surface Normals: A standard cylinder has a sharp \(90^\circ\) discontinuity between the side walls and the end caps. This requires either split vertices to maintain hard edges or creates distorted, pinched lighting if forced to share smooth normals. A capsule has continuously varying outward surface normals across its entire surface:

\[\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{p}) = \frac{\mathbf{p} - \text{proj}_L(\mathbf{p})}{\|\mathbf{p} - \text{proj}_L(\mathbf{p})\|}\]

When CapsuleGeometry is Preferred Over CylinderGeometry

1. Physics Engines and Character Controllers

The primary real-world use of CapsuleGeometry is in physics simulations (such as Rapier, Cannon-es, or PhysX bindings within Three.js apps).

Sharp edges on a CylinderGeometry cause "catching" artifacts when sliding against uneven terrain, steps, or mesh colliders. A capsule's spherical bottoms allow a character to smoothly slide over ground imperfections, curbs, and inclined planes without snagging. Additionally, calculating collisions against a swept sphere (capsule) requires only determining the distance between two line segments, which is computationally cheaper and less prone to numerical instability than resolving face-edge collisions on a cylinder.

2. High-Quality Lighting Without Shading Artifacts

Because CapsuleGeometry has \(C^1\) continuity, specular highlights smoothly glide over the ends of the object without the need for normal maps or post-processing bevels. When using metallic PBR materials (MeshStandardMaterial or MeshPhysicalMaterial), a capsule provides natural-looking edge roll-offs that look physically believable under directional lights and environment maps.

3. Organic Shapes and Rounded Components

Capsules are naturally suited for rendering objects that require rounded terminations without the vertex density of fully custom sculpted meshes. Common examples include:

  • Skeletal bone visualizers and ragdoll limbs.
  • Pills, capsules, and pharmaceutical modeling.
  • Rounded mechanical handles, soft buttons, and modern UI pill indicators.
  • Fast approximations of fingers, tails, and tentacles in procedural character models.

When to Stick with CylinderGeometry

CylinderGeometry remains the correct choice when your object strictly requires flat surfaces, such as coins, architectural pillars, pipes, wheels, or mechanical shafts. A cylinder also consumes fewer triangles when flat caps are needed, as it does not require the additional capSegments rings necessary to form smooth hemispherical domes.
